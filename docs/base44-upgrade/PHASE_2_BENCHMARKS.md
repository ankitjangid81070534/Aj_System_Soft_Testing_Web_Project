# Phase 2 — professional software-company pattern benchmark

Reviewed 2026-09-12 using public page text fetched from the sources below. This is a narrow **information architecture/content-pattern comparison**, not a visual design audit, performance comparison, verified customer-outcome study or conversion-rate experiment. Websites can change; observations describe the fetched text, not every interactive state. No external copy, logos, artwork, customer identities or outcome claims are imported into the app.

## 1. Atomic Object — project evidence organized by industry

Source: https://atomicobject.com/portfolio

Observed text:
- “All Case Studies” and “Filter by Industry.”
- Case-study cards describe a specific problem/product and expose a case-study link plus industry classification.
- Examples in the fetched page include hospital inventory with SxanPro, remote workforce communication with Root's Virtual Dialogue, and restaurant appliance reporting with 86 Repairs. These are **Atomic Object's displayed examples**, not AJS clients and not independently verified outcomes.

Pattern worth adapting: make project evidence easy to recognize as relevant to the visitor's workflow. Prefer concrete problem/product context to an unexplained technology/logo grid.

AJS application: retain real project name/summary/industry/platform and existing detail links. The existing `/projects` filter mechanism can serve available real facets; do not create fake records to enable filters. Improve project presentation only in later implementation phases with actual approved data.

Do not copy: client brands, case-study metrics or an implication of comparable portfolio depth. AJS currently has no configured real project dataset in this environment.

## 2. Thoughtworks — industry fit, relevant story and clear next steps

Source: https://www.thoughtworks.com/en-us/clients

Observed text:
- “Who we work with” and “Explore by industry.”
- Industry descriptions sit alongside routes to relevant client stories; examples include healthcare/life sciences and retail/ecommerce.
- Explicit “Get in touch” and “Our services” paths provide both conversation and service-discovery exits.

Pattern worth adapting: let a nontechnical buyer recognize their industry, then choose evidence, services or a conversation instead of navigating a technology taxonomy first.

AJS application: the existing Industries block and sector-specific service routes can orient clinic/retail visitors. The proposed movement ledger brings industry relevance earlier, subject to owner approval. Do not create enterprise-style industry landing pages or imply comparable specialist credentials without real content.

Do not copy: enterprise scale, years-in-business figures, AI claims, named client relationships or compliance implications. The fetched page is not evidence of AJS capability or measured visitor success.

## 3. Netguru — lifecycle explanation and service depth

Source: https://www.netguru.com/services

Observed text:
- “One-stop shop for digital” introduces services across the product lifecycle.
- Explicit sections include “Ideation and evaluation,” “Product design,” “Web development,” “Mobile development,” “AI, ML, and data science,” and “Support and management.”
- Service summaries link to deeper service pages; support is part of the lifecycle rather than an unexplained footer promise.

Pattern worth adapting: distinguish the buyer's business decision from the implementation detail; explain what happens before building and after launch.

AJS application: preserve all five current DeliveryProcess stages and source-ownership/support content. Use existing service detail routes for depth; do not cram the complete technical catalogue into the hero or remove current service descriptions as redundant.

Do not copy: workshop durations, staffing/service guarantees, slogans, unsupported technologies or their pricing/delivery claims. No recommendation here adds a new AJS service to the catalogue.

## Synthesis and limits

| Cross-site pattern | AJS planning decision | Explicit limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Relevant case studies carry problem/industry context | Keep real proof accessible after service/platform recognition | Empty data is not a license to manufacture proof |
| Business/industry language precedes technical detail | Use the seven persona decision paths; propose earlier industry fit | Proposed moves require approval and are not implemented |
| Service summaries lead to deep pages | Retain all existing 15 service slugs and the full index | Suggested cross-links are not claimed to exist already |
| Working process and ongoing support reduce unanswered questions | Preserve delivery stages, ownership and maintenance content | These are working principles, not measured outcomes or an SLA |
| Services, proof and contact offer distinct decisions | Preserve existing discovery, evidence and enquiry CTAs | Real enquiry success is blocked until integrations are verified |

Research did **not** establish that any particular section order converts better. The recommended order is a testable hypothesis for AJS, with a documented preservation/risk ledger rather than a competitor clone. No keyword-volume research, 5,000-query generation, analytics installation or external asset generation belongs to this phase.
