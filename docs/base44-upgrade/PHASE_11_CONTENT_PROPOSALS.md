## Small new-article proposals — approval required

### 1. Custom software vs off-the-shelf software

- **Potential URL (does not exist):** `/blog/custom-software-vs-off-the-shelf`.
- **Why this could be useful:** the existing custom-service description contrasts workflow-fit with adapting to packaged tools, but there is no substantial balanced comparison article in the current inventory.
- **Evidence:** `website/src/lib/data/services-fallback.ts`, custom-software long description, spreadsheet/manual-work problems and source-ownership FAQ.
- **Distinct question:** when is configuration of a packaged product enough, and when does bespoke development justify its responsibilities?
- **Outline:** workflow fit; configuration vs development; migration/integration constraints; ownership and maintenance responsibilities; how to compare total-cost assumptions without fabricated prices; questions for a discovery meeting.
- **Owner validation:** actual supported delivery models, contractual ownership wording, maintenance boundaries. Do not invent savings percentages, client examples, market-share comparisons or competitor shortcomings.
- **Links if approved:** existing custom-software service, planning article, quote page. The service retains commercial provider intent; this article owns the comparison question.
- **Go/no-go:** only if a reviewed comparison adds information beyond the existing service page; otherwise add a short balanced section there instead.

### 2. ERP vs CRM for a growing business

- **Potential URL (does not exist):** `/blog/erp-vs-crm-for-growing-businesses`.
- **Why this could be useful:** the existing ERP/CRM service grouping can leave buyers unsure which operational problem they actually need to solve.
- **Evidence:** current service SEO title names ERP and CRM, while its documented modules primarily describe sales, purchase, stock, reporting, permissions and migration. That is a reason to clarify, not license to invent CRM features.
- **Distinct question:** which requirements concern stock/purchasing/operations and which concern customer relationships, and when does integration matter?
- **Outline:** define the problem and users; compare requirements categories; overlapping data; staged adoption; integration and ownership questions; link to scoped discovery.
- **Owner validation:** confirm the actual CRM capabilities offered before presenting a CRM delivery checklist. No unsupported marketing automation, telephony, lead scoring, certification or named-product integration claims.
- **Links if approved:** ERP service, API integrations service, existing ERP digitisation article. Do not duplicate that article's four-stage rollout explanation.
- **Go/no-go:** no new article until business facts and distinct editorial value are approved; no separate CRM service landing page is justified by this research alone.

## Explicit exclusions

- **No new service page is currently justified:** all fifteen evidenced core service categories already have routes. Keyword combinations are not evidence that the company provides more services.
- Do not add AI development, city pages, new industry specialties, regulated certifications, payment products, fixed-price packages or testimonials just because a query could be generated.
- Do not create an extra cost article or requirements-download URL until extending the existing planning guide has been evaluated. One weakly differentiated article per modifier is not the goal.
- No `/hi` routes or hreflang declarations: a localization architecture and native-language review would be separate approved work.
- The proposed URLs above are **not** assigned as CSV owners, internal links in the live app or sitemap entries. Their spelling is provisional, not a URL rename request.
